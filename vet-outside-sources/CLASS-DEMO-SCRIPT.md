# Class demo: vetting outside material (8 minutes)

All material is invented. Nothing real is shown, run or opened.

- **The story (1 minute).** A classmate hands you a "helpful" prompt pack: forty prompts, a README, and a small helper tool. It looks great. You are about to paste it into your AI assistant and let it run. Ask the room: what would you check first? Take two answers, then move on.

- **What to show on screen (3 minutes).** Put up the invented BLOCKED summary, five lines, for ten seconds, then take it down and ask: use it or not? Then show it again and walk the lines.
  1. BLOCKED. Do not use.
  2. demo-notes-helper v1.2.0, a notes tool for an AI assistant.
  3. Found: text that talks to an AI (1 time); reads passwords or keys (2 times); runs on its own at startup (1 time).
  4. Not checked: what it does when it runs; 3 files too big to read.
  5. Next: the independent reviewer reads the 3 finds by Monday. Owner: reviewer. Nothing needed from you.

- **The one-line trust story (1 minute).** Say it once, plainly: "Good practice is to check every outside file before it runs, and to say what could not be checked." Point at line 4 of the summary. The not-checked line is the part that earns the trust.

- **Three discussion questions (2 minutes).**
  1. Which of the three finds would make you stop, and which would you want explained first?
  2. Line 4 says what was not checked. Would you rather have a report that hides that, or one that shows it? What changes in how you act?
  3. Who in your team should be the independent reviewer, and what should they never be allowed to clear on their own?

- **One honest caution (1 minute).** There is no measured result yet. This routine is adopted, not proven: nobody has counted how many bad items it caught or how many false alarms it raised. Offer the class the method, not a claim.
