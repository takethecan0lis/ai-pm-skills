# Decision digest

## Purpose
A status report that lists news and leaves the questions for the end gets skimmed, and the decisions in it stall. This skill turns the report into a page built around answers. The owner sees what they chose to see first, then what needs them, with enough context on each question to reply without opening anything else, and replies once.

## Objective and benefit
**Objective:** Each digest lets the owner see what happened, understand each decision, and answer all of them in one sitting on a phone.

**Benefit:** It aims to make decisions easier by giving every question its context, options, recommendation and the cost of waiting. It aims to cut reading by removing stale lines and asking nothing twice. A default and a deadline are meant to keep work moving when the owner is away. Without it, questions arrive without the facts needed to answer them, the owner has to hunt through other files, replies slip, and work waits on a decision nobody realized was theirs.

**Why it is worth having:** It aims at decisions made on time, from a page the owner trusts to be current. The outcome it is built for is less reading, fewer follow-up questions and fewer decisions that wait unseen.

## When to use
- You report periodically to someone who owns decisions about your work, and some of those decisions are open.
- Your updates run long, the owner skims them, and questions get missed or answered late.
- The owner reads on a phone and cannot open other documents to find the background.
- Skip it for a plain factual answer or a note with nothing to decide. A short message is better there.

## Personalize it: questions to ask first
Before you build the first digest, ask the person these questions. Each one is a personal choice: ask, do not assume. Ask one at a time, in plain words, offer the suggested default, and accept "use the default". Write the answers into a short preferences note (a template is in PREFERENCES-TEMPLATE.md) and read that note before every digest. If an answer changes later, update the note and say so.

First session: ask only questions 1, 5, 3 (with 2), 6 and 10. Use the defaults for the rest and ask them after the first digest.

1. Where and on what do you read it: phone, laptop, printed? (Suggested default: phone.)
2. How long should it take to read, in minutes? (Suggested default: 5 to 10.)
3. How many asks per area can you handle at once? (Suggested default: a small handful.)
4. What should come first: quick wins, decisions or risks? (No default; pick one.)
5. How do you want to answer: tap choices, short text, voice or a reply by message? (Suggested default: tap choices, with short text for comments.)
6. Which decisions do you always want to take yourself, so they never go ahead on a default? (No default. Ask the person to name the kinds of decision they always want to take themselves.)
7. How long may a default wait before it applies? (Suggested default: a few days, and never for a reserved decision.)
8. How often should a digest arrive, and at what time of day? (Suggested default: weekly, at a time you choose.)
9. How much detail do you like: full context under each question, or a short line with a link? (Suggested default: full context, because a reader on a phone often cannot follow a link.)
10. What must never appear on the page: private projects, names, amounts, anything else? (No default. List categories, not examples.)
11. How should past answers be kept and revisited? (Suggested default: stored beside each digest and reread before the next one.)
12. Do you want a metrics line, and which counts can be traced to a source? (Suggested default: no metrics line until a count can be traced.)
13. What tone and length do you want: plain and brief, warm, formal? (Suggested default: plain and brief outside question cards.)

If the person cannot answer yet, use the defaults, mark them as defaults in the note and ask again after the first digest.

## Steps
1. Before writing, read the previous digest and the answers the owner gave to it. Open the new digest with one short passage on what changed since those answers: what was acted on, what was decided by default, what is still open. If the previous digest is still unanswered, do not publish a second one. Update that page instead, so only one unanswered digest exists at a time.
2. Open with what the owner chose to see first (wins, decisions or risks), each stated plainly. Use real counts only when you can say where they came from. If a figure cannot be computed or traced, say that in the line rather than leaving it out or showing it as healthy.
3. Group the rest by project or area. In each area write three short parts in this order: what worked, what changed, and what needs the owner. Keep the first two brief, because the third is the reason the page exists.
4. Allow at most the number of asks per area set in the preferences note. Rank them by how much each blocks progress and keep the top ones. Anything you can settle yourself and reverse later, and that carries no outside risk, is not an ask: take the recommended answer, and list it in a "decided by default" line the owner can undo by naming it.
5. Write each ask so the decision comes first, in plain words, with any item number or label placed after it and explained beside it. Give the recommended answer straight after the decision, not a menu. State the deadline if one exists, and say what happens if the owner stays silent, such as which default is taken on which date.
6. Give each ask its full context directly under it: what happened, why it matters, the realistic options and what each would cause, your recommendation and why, and the risk of waiting. Write it so the owner could answer from the page alone. If a question needs another file to be understood, put the needed facts into the question and drop the link.
7. Mark reserved decisions clearly. A reserved decision is one the owner named in the preferences note as theirs alone. A reserved ask never gets a default, and the page says "reserved" next to it. Where one decision must follow another, say "after item N" so the owner answers in the right order.
8. Put a visible marker at the point where the owner can stop reading, so that everything above it is enough to act on. Place detail, history and supporting numbers below it.
9. Ask each thing once. If a question appears in an area card, it does not reappear in a summary, a list of waiting items or a closing note.
10. Remove stale content before publishing. Give every status line an as-of date, and delete or refresh any line you cannot date. A line you cannot vouch for should not be on the page.
11. Add a section headed "what this did not do or verify" before the closing line. List the areas not checked and the reason, and name anything reported as built or designed that has not been run. Never leave it empty.
12. Give each question its own answer box and each section a comment box, with a final box for anything else. Only questions get answer boxes. End the page with a single button the owner presses when done, so the digest records when the owner is done.
13. Store the finished digest and, when they arrive, the owner's answers and comments together in one place you can find again. The next digest reads them first, which is what step 1 depends on. A comment that would change a rule, a control or an outside system is confirmed with the owner before anyone acts on it.

## More rules that hold for any owner
- **Closing a digest.** When the owner says they have answered, read the answers, act on them, tell the owner what you made of them, and mark the digest closed. Carry any leftover into the next one. Silence does not close a digest, and an unread page is still open.
- **A new page while one is open.** If the owner asks for one anyway, say so, mark the old page as replaced with a visible note (never delete it) and carry its open items across.
- **No repeated questions.** Before publishing, check the earlier answers. If the owner already answered, state the answer and what was done. A request for instructions is not an answer: give the instructions, then ask once. A comment that is not an answer still gets a response.
- **Opening line of a question.** Say in plain words what it is about, then give one concrete example of when it matters. Define every label or code name in the same sentence.
- **Question card parts.** Cover why it is asked now, the history behind it, the options and their effects, the best argument against your pick, and the outcome on silence. Never trim a card for length; fold extra facts away instead.
- **Fresh-reader check.** Before publishing, ask of each question whether someone who never saw the thread could answer it. If not, add what is missing.
- **Answer controls.** Use radio choices for one answer among several, recommended first, and check boxes for picking several items, each with a select-all and a clear-all control. Use a text box only for free comments. Where an answer depends on a document, add a "not yet" choice, and do not count it as an answer.
- **Count before publishing.** Every question has a box, every box has a question, and no question appears twice.
- **States.** Show every state in words: no open questions ("Nothing needs you this time"), saving, saved, and failed ("Not saved. Your answers are kept on this device. Try again."). Keep drafts on the device. Above the done button, say how many questions are unanswered; do not block the button.
- **Summary at the top.** A few lines: what the page is, how many decisions are open, how long it takes, and the single most important point.
- **Settled without asking.** A short table of what you decided yourself, each with a one-line undo. Reserved decisions never appear in it.
- **Quick wins.** Put anything the owner can finish in a few minutes near the top, after the section the owner chose to see first, with any command or prompt in a copyable block.
- **Quick yes or no batch.** When the owner asks for fast calls, group up to about ten, each with its facts and a recommended answer.
- **Documents.** When an answer depends on a document, flag it inside the question, show short ones in full and let long ones expand in place. A reader on a phone cannot open files, so never rely on a file link. If a needed document cannot be shown, hold the question until it can, and label any edited copy as edited.
- **Safe handling.** Escape every inserted string, and show document text as plain text only. Scan the finished page for private names, paths, addresses and numbers; a hit drops the document with no override. Someone other than the author approves any edited copy. Never show secrets, other people's personal details or raw logs. Do not share a page that holds document text.
- **Page weight.** Tell the owner before publishing if the documents make the page very heavy.
- **Layout and checks.** Body text 16 px or more. Every tap target at least 44 by 44 px with space between choices. Text contrast at least 4.5 to 1 in both light and dark themes. No sideways scroll at 360 px wide. A visible focus ring on every control. Respect reduced motion. State shown by words or symbols as well as color. Reading order: summary, the section the owner chose first, areas, stop marker, detail, done button. Before rollout test a 360 px screen, both themes, every control and the counts.
- **Honest status.** Say designed, built or tested, and call nothing finished until it has been run. Label untested defaults as untested and remove an unverified note only when its check has run.
- **Versioned rules.** Keep a version number on your digest rules and reread them before each digest, so a running session picks up changes.

## Example
Invented data. Product team "Pebble Notes", digest dated 2026-03-09. Six lines of a sample outline.

1. Wins (as of 2026-03-09): onboarding checklist live for all new accounts since last Thursday. The count of finished signups comes from the signup log, which was readable.
2. Area card, Mobile app. Worked: crash reports fell after the last release. Changed: the offline mode release moved by one week. Needs you: one ask, below.
3. Stacked card, as a short block:
   Ask 1 (card). Decision: Ship offline mode to everyone on 2026-03-16, or to 10 percent first? Recommended: 10 percent first. Why: sync conflicts only show with real users. Choices: ( ) 10 percent first, full release slips a week; ( ) Everyone now, a bad conflict hits every note; ( ) Not yet. Deadline 2026-03-13. If silent: 10 percent goes out 2026-03-16.
4. Your answer box, followed by the area comment box.
5. You can stop reading here. Below this line is history and detail.
6. What this did not do or verify: sync behavior on tablets was not tested; the finished-signup count excludes accounts created before 2026-01-01 (as of 2026-03-09).

## What this does not verify
- It does not make the decisions better. It makes them easier to see and answer. A weak recommendation is still weak on a clear page.
- A long digest still gets skimmed. The cap on asks and the stop marker help, but they do not force reading.
- Owners may answer without reading the context. A silent owner triggers a default, and that default can be wrong.
- Stale facts mislead. The as-of dates only help if the author keeps them honest.
- Adopted, not measured: no result is claimed for faster answers, fewer follow-up questions or fewer missed decisions.

## Common failure modes
- Asks buried below the status text, so the owner finishes reading before reaching a question.
- Questions that need another file to answer, which a phone reader cannot open.
- Too many asks in one area, so the important one is lost among the minor ones.
- No default and no deadline, so a silent owner stalls the work.
- Counts nobody can trace, with no source named and no word on what could not be computed.
- Stale lines left in place, carrying no as-of date and still read as current.
- One digest piling onto another while the first is still unanswered, so the owner faces two pages of open questions.
- The same question asked in two places, which invites two different answers.
