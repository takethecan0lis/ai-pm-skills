# Finish first

## Purpose
Starting is cheap and finishing is not. This skill applies a work-in-progress limit to a person, with an assistant that is willing to say no. The owner opts in on purpose: they ask to be held to the limit, and the assistant keeps that promise even when the owner is in a hurry.

## Objective and benefit
**Objective:** Keep a small cap on open work and say no out loud to new work until something finishes.

**Benefit:** It aims at less half-built work, less context switching and fewer stale threads. New asks go to a visible backlog instead of quietly starting. Without a cap, it is easy to begin everything and finish little, and old threads can rot while attention jumps to the newest request.

**Why it is worth having:** It aims at more work actually finished, which is where value reaches users. It also gives a leader a reason to say no that both sides can see.

## When to use
- The owner has asked to be held to a limit of three open threads, and says so at the start.
- A new request arrives while three threads are open.
- Do not use it on work that carries no owner commitment, or on a request that is an emergency the owner names as one. Emergencies are an override, and they are logged like any other.

## Steps
1. Keep a short list of open threads. A thread is finished only when its result is checked and written down. "Built" is not finished.
2. When a new request arrives and three threads are open, do nothing on it first. Say plainly: "This breaks your finish-first cap."
3. List the open threads: name, age in days, what each is waiting on.
4. Offer the alternative: "Park this in the backlog instead?" If the owner agrees, collect only a one-line outcome, why now, rough size and at most three questions, write it down, and stop. No design or research on the parked item.
5. If the owner insists, challenge three times, one reason each, never repeating a reason: (a) what the new work displaces, (b) what is already waiting on the owner and the oldest open thread, (c) what the new work costs in time or money, including the cost of delay on the threads it pushes back.
6. Being annoyed, rushed, or calling the request small is not a reason to give in. Stay brief and friendly; challenge the work, not the person.
7. Give in only after the third challenge and a clear, repeated override. Then write the override in a log: date, the new work, and the thread it displaces.
8. Review parked and stale threads: a thread with no movement for two days goes to the owner as a question with a default of "park it".

## Example
Invented data. Owner is a product lead at "Pebble Notes". Open threads on 2026-03-10: (1) pricing page rewrite, age 9 days, waiting on legal copy; (2) onboarding checklist, age 4 days, waiting on test results; (3) churn survey, age 2 days, waiting on the owner's review.

Owner: "Add a referral program spec today."

Assistant: "This breaks your finish-first cap. Open threads: pricing page (9 days, legal copy), onboarding checklist (4 days, test results), churn survey (2 days, your review). Park the referral spec in the backlog instead?"

Owner: "No, it's small."
Challenge 1: "Small work still displaces something: the churn survey review you have not done."
Owner: "It's urgent."
Challenge 2: "The pricing page is your oldest thread at 9 days and is blocked on one reply. Unblocking it first frees a slot."
Owner: "Do it anyway."
Challenge 3: "A spec today delays the checklist release by about a day, and the checklist is the cheapest win waiting."
Owner: "Yes, start it, I'm sure."
Assistant logs: "2026-03-10, override: referral spec started; displaces churn survey review."

## What this does not verify
- It does not check that the three threads are the right ones, or that the cap of three is the right number for you.
- It depends on the owner keeping the thread list honest.
- Adopted, not measured: no result is claimed for fewer open threads or faster finishing.
